GLR Integrated Shipping Platform

LTL rate shopping · shipment dashboard · NetSuite integration · Interdisciplinary · SOAR

An integrated shipping platform for Great Lakes Radiant & Industrials, a mid-size industrial radiant heating distributor with five offices and a drop-ship origin in Warren, MI. The platform compares LTL freight rates across three brokers in real time, books shipments, writes freight cost data back to NetSuite, and sends branded tracking emails to customers. Built solo as Operations & Technology Lead.

Keywords: REST API integration, TypeScript, Cloudflare Workers, React, Vite, NetSuite SuiteScript, Supabase, logistics technology, LTL freight, rate optimization, middleware architecture, transactional email, PDF generation, data normalization, ERP integration, full-stack development.

Situation

GLR had no programmatic freight infrastructure. Sales reps manually checked rates through individual broker portals, shipments were booked by hand, customers received no tracking notifications, and freight cost data never made it back into NetSuite. With four active shipping origins and three freight brokers (Priority1, C.H. Robinson, R+L Carriers), every shipment was a manual, disconnected process.

Obstacle

Each broker had its own API, authentication model, and rate response schema. NetSuite's native tooling offered limited extensibility for real-time freight data. Building a unified system meant designing middleware that could speak to all three brokers simultaneously, normalize wildly different rate structures into a comparable format, handle async operations without blocking the UI, and log everything back to a central data store for reporting. There was no existing internal-tools infrastructure to build on, and I was the sole engineer.

Action

I designed and built a Cloudflare Worker middleware layer (TypeScript/Wrangler) as the integration hub. The rate-shopping engine calls Priority1, CHR, and R+L simultaneously, normalizes responses into a unified schema, and returns sorted results in under three seconds. A multi-step React/Vite shipment-creation form collects addresses, shipment type, freight details, and accessorial requirements, then hands off to the Worker for live rate fetching and booking confirmation. Supabase serves as the sync and logging layer: every shipment, rate fetch, and status event is recorded with broker attribution. On the NetSuite side, I built a SuiteScript integration that receives confirmed shipment data via webhook and writes carrier, PRO number, tracking status, and freight cost back to the corresponding Sales Order record. Resend handles transactional email: dispatch confirmations and a branded customer-facing tracking page that resolves PRO numbers through the appropriate broker's tracking API. I also designed a GLR-branded BOL document using ReportLab, giving the company a consistent document across all three brokers rather than pulling PDFs from three different portals.

To validate the business case, I ran a 400-lane rate comparison across all four shipping origins, analyzing same-carrier matchups between brokers to isolate margin differences from network gaps. The analysis revealed CHR beats Priority1 on 73% of same-carrier lanes by an average of $143 per shipment, with P1's 33.3% flat markup as the primary driver. That data directly informed a vendor negotiation in progress.

Result

The platform is deployed and in active use. The three-way rate shopper produces an estimated $16,900 in annual savings across three offices compared to the current single-broker setups, with additional upside from the drop-ship origin. Freight cost data flows into NetSuite automatically, eliminating manual entry. Customers receive branded tracking emails for the first time in company history. The work also produced a concrete, data-backed negotiation posture with Priority1 around API fee structure and rate competitiveness.
