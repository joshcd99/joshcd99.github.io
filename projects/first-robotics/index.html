FIRST Robotics · Technical Mentor

Mentor since Sept 2019 · 6+ years · Outside CS · Service / Leadership

I've been a technical mentor for FIRST Robotics since September 2019. It's the longest-running thread in my life outside of family and school. Each season I guide students on software-hardware integration, teach Java programming and embedded-systems concepts, and help organize and run the competitions that make the program work.

I'm cataloging this as outside CS because the mentorship and service core of the work is overwhelmingly non-coding: it's pedagogy, patience, project management with teenagers under deadline, judging and event logistics, parent communication, and the slow craft of helping a fifteen-year-old believe they can be an engineer. The Java is the easy part. FIRST is also the reason I'm a CS major, which makes mentoring it feel less like volunteering and more like paying down a debt.

Keywords: FIRST Robotics, mentorship, service, Java, embedded systems, software-hardware integration, STEM education, deadline-driven, teaching, leadership, six-year commitment, common good.

What I do

  • Guide students on software-hardware integration. It's the place where most rookie teams get stuck, and where the line between a working robot and a parked robot is drawn.
  • Teach Java programming and embedded-systems concepts to students who, for many of them, have never written code before the season starts.
  • Support and help organize FIRST competitions, ensuring smooth operations and a positive experience for participants.
  • Show up. Repeatedly. For six years and counting: through high school, through college, through summer internships.

Why it matters here

Mentoring FIRST is where the human-centered piece of my engineering practice was forged. Engineering is a team discipline before it's a technical one. A brilliant solution shipped late is worse than a competent solution shipped on time. The person on your team you don't get along with usually has information you need. "The robot is built by the team that builds the team." Those framings show up in everything I do now: how I scope features at Great Lakes, how I run code review, how I talk to a sales engineer before writing a line of integration code, even how I think about who should be in the room when a model gets deployed. Six years of mentoring teenagers is, it turns out, excellent preparation for every adult engineering team I've ever worked on.

Also currently mentoring through Big Brothers Big Sisters as a one-on-one youth mentor (April 2025–present), a smaller commitment in hours but a much larger commitment in attention.
